Understanding Bullying

Know the four types of bullying

Bullying takes many forms. Recognizing each type is the first step to stopping it.

👊

Physical Bullying

Involves bodily harm or threats, damaging belongings, or forcing someone to do something against their will.

  • Hitting, kicking, or pushing
  • Stealing or damaging property
  • Threatening gestures
🗣️

Verbal Bullying

Using words to hurt, intimidate, or demean. Often dismissed as "just words," but the emotional impact is real and lasting.

  • Name-calling and teasing
  • Taunting and insults
  • Threats and intimidation
🤝

Social / Relational Bullying

Aimed at damaging someone's reputation or relationships, often subtle and hard to detect by adults.

  • Excluding someone deliberately
  • Spreading rumors or lies
  • Public humiliation
📱

Cyberbullying

Bullying that happens online or through digital devices — it follows victims home, making it impossible to escape.

  • Harassing messages or posts
  • Sharing private photos
  • Creating fake accounts
Warning Signs

How to tell if someone is being bullied

Many victims don't speak up. Knowing the signs means you can step in before it gets worse.

A child who was once outgoing may become withdrawn, anxious, or irritable. Watch for sudden personality shifts, increased sadness, or unexplained mood swings — especially after school or screen time.
Frequent complaints of headaches, stomachaches, or feeling sick — especially on school days — can be a sign that a child is trying to avoid a situation at school. They may fake illness or beg to stay home.
Torn clothing, unexplained bruises, or missing belongings — especially when the child can't give a clear explanation — may be signs of physical bullying. Take these signs seriously and follow up gently.
Suddenly stopping device use, appearing upset after going online, hiding screens, or being secretive about online activity can signal cyberbullying. Don't punish the device — create a safe space to talk.
A child who no longer seems to have friends, stops being invited to events, or avoids group activities may be experiencing social exclusion — one of the most painful and overlooked forms of bullying.

If you see bullying, act.

Bystanders have enormous power to stop it. Here's what you can do right now:

1

Don't ignore it

Silence sends the message that bullying is acceptable. Your voice makes a difference.

2

Support the target

A simple "I'm here for you" can break the isolation a victim feels. Check in privately.

3

Report to a trusted adult

Tell a parent, teacher, or counselor. Reporting is not "tattling" — it's protecting someone.

4

Don't retaliate

Fighting back escalates the situation. Focus on getting safe and getting help instead.

How to Help

Guidance for everyone

Whether you're a student, parent, or educator — everyone has a role to play in stopping bullying.

🤝

Be an upstander

When you see bullying, speak up. Stand beside the person being targeted and let them know they're not alone. Your bravery encourages others to do the same.

💬

Tell a trusted adult

If you or someone you know is being bullied, tell a teacher, parent, or school counselor. Don't carry it alone — adults can take action that you can't.

❤️

Be kind online

Think before you post. If you wouldn't say it to someone's face, don't type it. Use your social media presence to uplift, not tear down.

👂

Listen without judgment

Create a safe space where your child feels comfortable sharing. Avoid dismissing their experience or placing blame — just listen and validate.

🏫

Work with the school

Contact teachers and administrators. Document incidents with dates and details. Request a meeting to create a safety plan and follow up regularly.

📱

Monitor online activity

Use parental controls and keep devices in common areas. Teach your child about digital safety, and check in regularly about their online relationships.

📋

Enforce clear policies

Establish and consistently enforce zero-tolerance anti-bullying policies. Make sure every student understands the consequences and where to report incidents.

🧭

Build an inclusive culture

Foster empathy and respect through classroom activities, discussions, and modeling kind behavior. Celebrate differences and create belonging for every student.

🔍

Watch for warning signs

Learn to recognize behavioral and emotional signals. Intervene early, check in with students who seem isolated, and connect them with counseling resources.
